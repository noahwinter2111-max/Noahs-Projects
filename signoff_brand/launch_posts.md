## 7. Guest article

Title: What it takes to start a company in 2026, step by step

Here is the list, in the order it has to happen, with the waiting built in.

Day 1. Pick the entity. A company that will ever raise from investors is a Delaware C corporation. One that never will is an LLC in its home state. That one question decides it.

Day 1. File the formation. Delaware takes a few days. Some states take weeks. Nothing else can start until the certificate comes back.

Day 4 to 10. Apply for the EIN. Free on the IRS site, about fifteen minutes, weekdays only. Nobody needs to be paid for this.

Day 4 to 10. Operating agreement or bylaws. Nobody reads it until there is a disagreement. It gets written anyway.

Day 5 to 15. Open the bank account. The slowest step. The bank wants the certificate, the EIN letter, the agreement, an ID and a one-line reason. Two days to two weeks.

Day 15. Connect the books. Connected to the bank on the account's first day, not month three.

Day 15. Email on the domain. Ten minutes.

Day 16 onward. The part that never ends. Invoices, bills, quarterly estimated tax, the state annual report, Delaware franchise tax, 1099s in January.

Three things worth knowing.

The chain is the problem, not the steps. Each step is easy. Remembering which one is pending is where a month goes.

Reserve for tax from the first dollar. A fixed share of every deposit goes to a separate account that is never touched. Skipping this is regretted in April.

Decide what software is never allowed to do. Holding money and signing are the usual answers. Everything else can be delegated if it can be audited.

Signoff runs this chain from one sentence and then keeps the books, with an approval step on anything outward. It is a prototype right now: https://signoff-three.vercel.app

## 8. Reddit replies

Answers to real threads. Only the last one mentions Signoff, and only when someone asks what tool to use.

"Do I need an LLC or a C corp?"
One question decides it. Will the company ever raise money from investors who expect equity? If yes, Delaware C corp, because every investor's paperwork assumes it and converting later costs more. If no, an LLC in the home state is cheaper, simpler and taxed more kindly. A Delaware or Wyoming LLC for someone who lives elsewhere ends up registered at home anyway, paying twice.

"Should I pay a service to get my EIN?"
No. Free on the IRS site, about fifteen minutes. The site is only open on weekdays during US business hours, which is why it seems hard. The formation certificate has to exist first. Paid services fill in the same form.

"Bank keeps rejecting my business account application."
Usually one of three things. The name on the certificate does not exactly match the application. The EIN letter is missing, and banks want the actual IRS letter, not the number. Or the business description is vague. One sentence saying what is sold and to whom, plus all three documents attached before applying, fixes most of it.

"How do I not get killed by taxes in year one?"
A second account. A fixed share of every deposit moves into it the day the money lands, and it is never touched. Quarterly estimates get paid from it. The percentage depends on state and margins and is a question for an accountant. Trouble comes from treating the whole balance as spendable.

"Is there a tool that does all of this?"
Signoff. Describe the business, it forms the company, gets the EIN, opens the account and connects the books, then runs the back office with an approval step on anything outward. It is a prototype with the partners simulated right now, so not something to run a real company on today. The flow is at signoff-three.vercel.app.
