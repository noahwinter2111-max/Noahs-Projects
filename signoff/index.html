Two kinds of company. One back office.

The first question decides which path you're on. Everything after that is the same chat.

Building the next big thing

"I'll raise money"

Say you'll raise money and Signoff sets you up the way investors expect, on day one.

  • Delaware C corporation, registered in your home state too.
  • 10,000,000 founder shares issued at formation.
  • Post-money SAFEs drafted on the standard form and sent for signature.
  • Cap table that updates the moment the wire lands.
  • Data room links that expire, monthly investor updates, runway.

  • QuickBooks with the bank feed on, invoices that chase themselves.

02 · How it works

What happens, in order.

You describe the business in your own words. Signoff does the rest in this sequence, and each step only unlocks when the one before it is actually done.

  1. 01 · Intake
    Intake

    You say what you do, where you live, and whether you'll raise money. A freelancer in Texas gets a single-member Texas LLC. A founder who says "I'll raise" gets a Delaware C corporation with founder shares, registered in Texas as well. It explains the choice in two sentences and declines businesses the bank won't take: money services, gambling, cannabis, weapons, crypto exchanges.

  2. 02 · Identity
    Identity

    A photo of your ID, a selfie and a sanctions screen, run by the identity partner. Signoff never sees or stores your ID number. The check is repeated every 90 days in the background.

  3. 03 · Form + EIN
    Formation and EIN

    It drafts the filing and shows you a card with the name, entity, state and fee. You approve, the filing partner submits it, and the EIN application follows once the state accepts. Both land in the Company panel.

  4. 04 · Bank
    Bank account

    The certificate, EIN letter and operating agreement go to the bank. The bank runs its own KYC and opens the account. Signoff waits for the bank's "account open" message before anything downstream unlocks.

  5. 05 · Books + email
    Books and email

    QuickBooks Online connects with the bank feed on. Gmail or Microsoft 365 connects next. From here, "invoice Acme $4,500 net 14" and "what came in this week" are ordinary requests.

  6. 06 · Run
    Running the company

    Payments, invoices, travel on the company card, replies to customers, and a compliance calendar with the annual report and tax dates. A CPA is matched before your first filing is due.

  7. 07 · Raise
    Raising money

    Say who and how much. If you started as an LLC, Signoff converts it to a Delaware C corporation first, only with your approval. Then it issues a standard post-money SAFE for e-signature, watches for the wire, keeps the cap table, shares a read-only data room that expires, and sends investors a monthly update you sign off on.

03 · Who decides

The agent proposes. The gate decides.

The model proposes. A policy gate written in plain code decides what each action needs. The model can't argue its way past it, and neither can a prompt hidden in an email.

What Signoff does

  • Reads your inbox, books and balance on its own.
  • Drafts filings, invoices, emails and bookings, then waits for your tap.
  • Sends a payment to the bank only after you type the six-digit code.
  • Keeps a log of every proposal, approval and decline.
  • Issues SAFEs, keeps the cap table and sends investor updates when you raise.

What it will not do

  • Hold your money. The bank holds it; Signoff only sends instructions.
  • Sign anything for you. Every filing and payment carries your approval.
  • Ask for your SSN, ID number or bank password. The partners collect those directly.
  • Pay a new payee more than $1,500, or more than your daily limit, without a passkey.
  • Issue a SAFE with a cap under four times the amount, or over $5M. Those go to a lawyer.

05 · Questions

Things people ask before they sign up.

Is this a real product?

Not yet. This page is a working prototype of the product. The conversation runs on a script by default, or on Claude if you add your own API key in Settings. The formation partner, identity check, bank, QuickBooks and Gmail are all simulated so you can click through the whole journey in about three minutes.

Why does the bank do the identity check instead of you?

Because the bank has to do it anyway, by law, and it's liable for the result. Doing it twice costs money and annoys the owner. Signoff sends the bank the documents it needs and waits for the bank's answer.

What stops the model from doing something it shouldn't?

It can only act through a fixed list of tools, and each tool goes through a gate written in ordinary code. The gate knows which stage you're at, what tier the action is, and what approval it needs. Read-only actions run freely. Reversible writes run and are logged. Anything that leaves the company, like a filing, an email or an invoice, waits for your tap. Anything that moves money also needs a one-time code, and the bank's own checks sit on top of that.

What does it cost to run?

The expensive part is the model, so the agent only gets the tools for the stage you're in and keeps its answers short. A typical setup conversation is a few hundred thousand tokens end to end. Partner fees, like the state filing fee, are passed through at cost and shown on the card before you approve.

Can I bring my own bank or accountant?

Later. The first version works with one bank and QuickBooks Online, because a single integration that works end to end is worth more than four that half work. Gmail and Microsoft 365 are both supported for email from the start.

Is this for startups or small businesses?

Both, and the difference is one answer at the start. A founder who plans to raise gets a Delaware C corporation with founder shares, because that's what investors and SAFEs expect, and pays Delaware franchise tax for it. Everyone else gets a home-state LLC, which is cheaper and simpler. If a small business later decides to raise, Signoff converts it, with your approval.

Who is building this?

Evan Winter, founder of Truffles AI, a credit-intelligence platform used by 13 financial institutions, with his family. He has set up companies by hand before and wants the next one to take an afternoon. Truffles reads tax returns, financials and ledgers for lenders; Signoff is the same idea pointed at the owner's side of the desk.
